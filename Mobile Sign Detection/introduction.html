Introduction to Sign Detection

Understanding the problem before looking at the computer vision techniques used to solve it.

Why Is Sign Detection Important?

Signs are found throughout the environments people use every day. Examples include exit signs, bathroom signs, room numbers, elevator signs, and directional signs.

For a person who is blind or has low vision, finding these signs can be difficult because they may not be easy to locate without visual information. A mobile camera combined with computer vision could help identify signs in the surrounding environment.

However, simply being able to read text from an image is not enough. Before the text can be read, the system first needs to determine whether a sign is present and where it is located.

Detecting a Sign vs. Reading a Sign

It is important to separate sign detection from sign reading. These are two different computer vision problems.

Sign Detection

The system looks at an image and determines whether a sign is present and where the sign is located.

For example, a computer vision model could draw a bounding box around an exit sign.

Sign Reading

After a sign has been located, OCR or another recognition method can be used to determine what text appears on it.

For example, OCR could read the word "EXIT" from a detected exit sign.

The Sign Detection Pipeline

A mobile sign-reading system can contain several steps. The important part of this tutorial is the step where the system finds and locates the sign.

1 - Camera

A smartphone camera captures the surrounding environment.

↓

2 - Image Frame

The camera provides an image or video frame for analysis.

↓

3 - Detection

The computer vision system searches for signs in the frame.

↓

4 - Location

A bounding box identifies where the sign is located.

↓

5 - Identification

The system can determine the type of sign that was detected.

↓

6 - Optional OCR

OCR can be used to read text after the sign has been found.

↓

7 - User Information

The system can provide information about the detected sign.

The Main Challenge

Detecting a sign is relatively straightforward when the sign is large, clearly visible, facing the camera, and separated from the background.

Real-world environments are much more difficult. A sign may be far away, very small, viewed from an angle, partially blocked by another object, affected by lighting, or surrounded by many other objects and signs.

This leads to the main question of this tutorial:

How can computer vision detect and locate signs from a mobile camera when the signs are viewed from different angles, partially blocked, far away, small, or surrounded by complex backgrounds?

What Makes Sign Detection Difficult?

Different Angles

A sign may not face the camera directly. Perspective can change the shape and appearance of the sign.

Occlusion

Objects or people may partially block a sign, leaving only part of the sign visible.

Small or Distant Signs

A sign that is far away may only occupy a small number of pixels in the camera image.

Complex Backgrounds

Other objects, decorations, windows, and printed text can make it harder to distinguish a sign from its surroundings.

Lighting

Bright light, shadows, darkness, and reflections can change how a sign appears in an image.

Camera Movement

Movement can cause blur and make signs more difficult to detect, especially when the camera is being used while walking.
